The Web Conference 2020
General Co-Chairs’ Welcome
It is our great pleasure to welcome you to The Web Conference 2020, Taipei. This is the 29th edition of the
conference series, and also the 30-year celebration of the inception of the Web. The Web Conference is the
premier international venue focused on understanding the current state and the evolution of the Web through
the lens of computer science, computational social science, economics, policy, and many other disciplines. It
is a place where researchers, practitioners, industrialist, inventors, technologists, and the alike to present and
discuss progress in research, development, standards, and applications related to the Web. The 2020 edition
of the conference is a reflection on the past 30 years of the Web, the latest research findings, and on the future
of Web-based technologies. The conference is organized under the aegis of the International World Wide
Web Conference Committee (IW3C2) in collaboration with local conference organizers in the wonderful host
city of Taipei.
The technical program of this year’s edition of the conference consists of three distinguished keynotes by
world renowned experts: Yolanda Gil of USC, Sir Nigel Shadbolt of Oxford, and Wei-Ying Ma of
Bytedance, 12 tracks for refereed research papers including full papers and short papers, and a special track
“Future of the Web” that are published in the main volume of the proceedings. The companion volume of
the proceedings includes 14 workshops, 12 tutorials, and posters. We also have several other co-located
events and special tracks: W4A (Web for All), BIG (Big Data Innovators Gathering), W3C (World Wide Web
Consortium), The Web of Health, AIoT (AI of Things), Web@30, and The Future of the Web. All these events
highlight the active and vigorousness of our Web community!

The Web Conference 2020 Research Chairs’ Preface
The research program of this year’s edition consists of 12 tracks for refereed research papers
(including full papers and short papers), and a special track “Future of the Web.” As in previous
years, updates were made to ensure that track topics correctly reflect the key research areas of
interest to the Web community.
The research tracks attracted submissions from 5649 distinct authors from 60 countries spanning
six continents. Submissions were rigorously reviewed by a program committee (PC) comprising
26 track chairs, 136 senior PC members (on six of the tracks), and 1001 PC members and 608
external reviewers. A total of 5847 reviews were provided, with each paper receiving at least
three reviews, and many papers receiving four or more reviews.
During the discussion phase, PC members, senior PC members, and track chairs discussed the
reviews in detail. Meta-reviews were provided for most submissions, especially for borderline
papers and high-scoring rejects, summarizing the reviews and discussions. The rebuttals from
the authors were also seriously considered.
For consistency across tracks, final decisions on papers were made at the face-to-face PC meeting
between track chairs and the PC chairs. We accepted 219 (19%) of the 1129 full papers and 97
(24%) of the 397 short papers submitted to the research tracks (which also include a small number
of short papers converted from full papers). New this year, all full and short papers were
presented orally. The decision to accept a paper was entirely based on quality and not its length.
As said, a special track called “Future of the Web” was organized as part of the research program.
In total, 11 invited talks were accepted. The extended abstracts of some of these talks have also
been included in the conference proceedings.
We would like to take this opportunity to thank all the authors for submitting their papers to our
conference, thank all the track chairs, senior program committee members, and reviewers for
their hard work. We hope you will enjoy the research program of the conference!
Tie-Yan Liu and Maarten van Steen
Program Co-Chairs, TheWebConf 2020
