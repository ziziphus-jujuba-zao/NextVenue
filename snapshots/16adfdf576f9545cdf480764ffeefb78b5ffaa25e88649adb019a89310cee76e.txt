Centering the Human: Communication
in an Age of Uncertainty
The theme for ICA 2027 invites scholars to reflect on the nature and aims of communication
research in a time shaped by the transformative impact of artificial intelligence, and set against
the backdrop of intensifying global crises, growing authoritarianism and democratic
backsliding, geopolitical instability, and climate emergency.
Artificial intelligence is rapidly reshaping communication processes, societal institutions
(including higher education), and research practices across the globe. From algorithmic
curation of social media platforms to the use of Large Language Models for communication
practices and scholarship, AI is transforming how communication is produced, circulated,
researched, and governed.
These developments raise fundamental questions about the place of human agency in
communication. They require us to reflect on the aims and preoccupations of communication
research, accounting for technological change without losing our inherent focus on how
humans communicate, and the role of traditional media organizations and platforms within this
process.

Unequal access to AI and disparate impacts of the technology and its infrastructure
throughout the world bring a new dimension to ongoing challenges of expanding
communication research and growing a more inclusive community of scholars. These
questions are both normative and practical, and reverberate across the varied fields and areas
of research represented by ICA divisions and interest groups.
At the same time, the uncertainties confronting communication are not reducible to
technological change alone. We are living through a period marked by overlapping and
compounding forms of uncertainty. These include, but are not limited to, geopolitical conflict
on a global scale; environmental crisis and climate precarity; economic instability and
widening inequalities; and epistemic uncertainty in the form of misinformation, challenges to
academic freedom and scholarly expertise, and declining trust in institutions, including
universities and legacy media organizations. These conditions represent challenges for
individuals, groups, civic society actors, and media organizations - as well as for
communication researchers. The transformations we are witnessing are experienced unevenly
across the world. This theme therefore invites broader reflection on the role of communication
and communication scholarship, in understanding and addressing the challenges of our time.
To center the human in communication research means foregrounding lived experience and
human agency in mediated communication, while coming to terms with the growing power of
automated agents within communication processes. It means critically theorizing the impact
of these transformations, informed by normative commitments. But it also entails examining
how our engagement with ever-changing technologies shapes everyday communicative
practices and institutional structures. It ultimately invites reflection on the role of scholars,
methods, and epistemologies in shaping knowledge about communication under conditions
of uncertainty.
We encourage submissions that examine how communication is evolving in this context,
across diverse global settings. Submissions may address, but are not limited to, the following
questions:
What new methodological and theoretical frameworks are needed to study
communication in rapidly changing environments?
How do technological, geopolitical, environmental, or epistemic uncertainties shape
communication practices and institutions?
How is artificial intelligence reconfiguring human agency, creativity, and labor in
communication? What are the main challenges and opportunities of these
transformations?
In what ways are communication systems implicated in democratic backsliding,
authoritarianism, polarization, and struggles over trust and legitimacy?
How are conditions of uncertainty unevenly experienced and mediated across different
social and global contexts, and what role does communication play in structuring these
inequalities?
What are the implications of global crises (including climate change, conflict and health
emergencies) for human communication, broadly understood?
How do cultural, affective, and embodied dimensions of communication shape
experiences of, and responses to, uncertainty?
What role can communication scholars play in fostering more just, inclusive, and
sustainable futures?
