Opening Matters

The 2022 ACM SIG IR Annual Business Meeting took place as a hybrid event during the 45th
International ACM SIGIR Conference on Research and Development in Information Retrieval
in Madrid, Spain on July 14, 2022. The business meeting was led by the chair of the SIG IR
Executive Committee (EC), Vanessa Murdock.
The chair is thanking the conference organizers of SIGIR 2022 for planning this fantastic event.
The general chairs are Enrique Amigó, Pablo Castells, Julio Gonzalo. The program chairs are
Ben Carterette, Shane Culpepper, Gabriella Kazai. Special thanks is directed to the Student
volunteers.
This SIGIR conference was held as a hybrid event, with 1049 registered attendees from 47
countries overall, and 564 attendees on site. Acceptance rates were 20% for full research papers,
and 25% across all tracks. For the second time, it was co-located with the ACM International
Conference on Theory in Information Retrieval (ICTIR). Several firsts from the previous conferences were continued, such as Perspective, resource, and industry (SIRIP) tracks, presentations
of papers from the TOIS journal. For the first time, SIGIR is offering a track for reproducibility
papers.

2.3

SIGIR Test of Time

At SIGIR, the Test of Time Award recognizes research that has had long-lasting influence, including impact on a subarea of information retrieval research, across subareas of information retrieval
research, and outside of the information retrieval research community. The winning paper is
selected from the set of full papers presented at the main SIGIR conference 10–12 years before.
The 2022 ACM SIGIR Test of Time Award went to the SIGIR 2012 paper Modeling the
impact of short- and long-term behavior on search personalization by Paul Bennett, Ryen White,
Wei Chu, Susan Dumais, Peter Bailey, Fedor Borisyuk, Xiaoyuan Cui.1
The 2022 ACM SIGIR Test of Time Honorable Mention went to the SIGIR 2010 paper
on Uncovering social spammers: social honeypots + machine learning by Kyumin Lee, James
Caverlee, Steve Webb.2
The active members of the selection committee were, in alphabetical order: Hannah Bast
(chair), Pablo Castells, Yiqun Liu, Alistair Moffat, Berthier Ribeiro-Neto, Tetsuya Sakai, and
Emine Yilmaz.

Treasurer’s Report

SIG IR treasurer Carsten Eickhoff presented SIG IR’s finances. Overall, the previous treasurer
Min Zhang has ensured that our finances are in a healthy state. The available funds fluctuate as
sponsored events need to be financially supported and profits are returned. This is especially true
for larger conferences. The income is relatively constant since 2017, but expenses have dropped
during the pandemic years. In the years before the pandemic, a big part of the yearly expenses
were student travel grants (more than USD 200,000 across a number of sponsored conferences).
With less travel and with more conferences being held online, there was less spending, but we
believe this will return soon to pre-pandemic levels.
There are currently USD 1.36M available in the account to underwrite conferences and support
community endeavors (See Figure 1).

4.1

Expenses

The fiscal year 2022 covers the period from July 1st, 2021 till June 30th, 2022.4 The major
contributor to expenses are travel grants. While these were reduced during the pandemic, with
USD 125,000 projected for 2022, we expect these expenses to sharply incline now that students
are travelling to conferences in-person.
There are several other posts, such as volunteer meetings and travel, costs for online conferences, publication expenses (e.g. proceedings of the SIGIR conference), awards, and special
projects (more below). Somewhat new is the post for professional services, which includes the
professional rebranding of the SIGIR website.
One of the special projects is the financial support via the “SIG IR & Friends” funding for
workshops, local research gatherings, etc. Interested parties can apply for financial support online.5
For fiscal year 2022, USD 9,000 were issued.
Similarly, SIG IR Chapters can be established whenever there is a strong local base of IR
researchers that would benefit from local organization. Interested groups can apply to the ACM
to become a local SIG IR Chapter.
Additional special projects are DEI grants to increase the diversity among attendees and
funding for DEI and student socials at conferences.

4.2

4.3

Conferences co-funded by SIG IR

The ACM SIG IR special interest group has a stake in several related conferences. This includes
SIGIR (the conference), CIIR, ICTIR, CIKM (50%, co-funded by SIG WEB), AFIRM (50%, cofunded with SIG KDD), JCDL (34%), and WSDM (25%, co-funded by SIG KDD, SIG WEB, and
SIG MOD).
Having a stake in a conference means to both financially support/underwrite it, but also to
obtain a share of the profits. Usually the SIGIR conference obtains the highest profits, with CIKM
being second.
Overall there is a slight decline in SIG IR memberships, but the conference stakes, chapters,
and events funded via SIG IR & Friends is increasing. Feedback about where more (or less) funds
should be allocated should be communicated to the treasurer.

SIGIR Conferences

Reports from past, future, and potential future conferences are presented.

5.1

SIGIR ’21 recap: Virtual/Montreal

Despite (or because) of being virtual SIGIR ’21 was attended by 973 participants from 46 countries.
This conference was organized by general chairs Fernando Diaz, Chirag Shah, and Thorsten Suel,
with program chairs Pablo Castells, Rosie Jones, and Tetsuya Sakai. The conference was run with
support from Underline, using Zoom and Gather.Town.
The conference was switched to an online format in the last moment, as the organizers had
sincerely hoped to offer an in-person conference. The SIGIR 21 conference has several firsts,
including resource papers and perspectives papers, where the latter are designed to discuss topical
areas that deserve more attention. An increased amount of DEI efforts were dedicated to financially
enabling wide-spread participation, including the provision of family care grants. The first Queer
in AI meeting was held at SIGIR ’21. The inaugural class of the SIGIR Academy was presented
here. It was that for the first time, SIGIR was co-located with ICTIR.
The conference yielded as much surplus as its expenses. About the same amount was obtained
from sponsors.

6.3

New: ACM SIG IR Early Career Researcher (ECR) Awards

A new award is created to recognize the excellence of our early career members. Eligible awardees
must be within seven years of obtaining a doctoral degree. Awards are granted in the following
three categories.
Research: To honor single or cumulative research contributions to the field of IR.
Community Engagement: To honor community engagement and professional service to the
community.
Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion: To honor individuals that are initiating driving, and are
supporting diversity, equity, and inclusion efforts in the community.
The ACM SIG IR ECR awards will be selected by a panel of five committee members. The
processes will follow the ACM policy on diversity and inclusion as well as conflict of interest.
Nominations and self-nominations across multiple categories are permitted. The award is open
to anyone who is a current SIG IR member and an active participant. Nomination deadline is December 31st, 2022. Details can be found online6 and will be announced on IRList. Questions should
be directed to the ACM SIG IR ECR awards committee chair via sigir-ecr-award@acm.org.

suggestions were made and questions were raised:
• Food options and restrictions. A friendly request to designate a role to work with local
arrangement/catering to convey a range of food restrictions people have and how buffets
could be labeled accordingly.

of COVID sensitivities and to consider health as a DEI factor.
• Perspective papers. Positive comments and applause.
• Presenters missing during Q&A. Frustration was voiced about speakers with pre-recorded

to connect to a zoom call in real time in their time zone. We should assume good intent and
find out what the reasons were so we can address them. It is on the conference organizers
to reduce the friction for presenters.
• Pre-recorded videos. Questions were raised about avoiding in-person sessions comprised
solely of pre-recorded videos. It is noted that some time-zones make participation difficult.
A suggestion was made to collect and share information about which presentations will be
pre-recorded.
• Scheduling issues with ICTIR (and other co-located events). Parallel scheduled sessions,
forced attendees had to decide between keynotes and technical sessions, and leaving some
technical sessions with less than desirable number of attendees. Better synchronization
across co-located parallel events is desirable.
• Virtual day. The SIGIR virtual day was held the week before the in-person conference, but
not many people attended. The experience should be improved and better integrated into
the main event.
• Long-term vs short-term engagement. A discussion about how conferences (unlike journals)
are for community building, and how long-term discussions could be further facilitated online. Second screen experiences should not be a lesser kind of engagement, and we should
leverage existing online options to build a stronger online/offline integration for the SIG IR
community.
Additional discussions on the Slack channel evolve around
• Review Model. Better reviewing guidelines for less frustrating reviews. Rolling Review or
Journal-style review.
• What is in scope for IR? Obtaining convergence on which topics are acceptable at IR conferences and communicating this. The goal is to avoid reliance on reviewers on notions but
to establish criteria with the input from program chairs.
• Encouraging a consistent manuscript page lengths across conferences.
• Suggestion to establish a self-nomination process for PC organizing positions
