We are delighted to welcome you to the 14 ACM International Conference on Web Search and
Data Mining (WSDM 2021). WSDM is one of the premier conferences in the fields of web search
and data mining, with a dynamic and growing community from academia and industry. Our
original plan was to meet face-to-face at Jerusalem, Israel, however, given the COVID
circumstances, the conference is given virtually. We hope you enjoy the conference, learn a lot,
meet old friends, and make many new connections.
This year the program for WSDM 2020 will begin with Tutorials, Industry Day, and the Doctoral
Consortium. The ten tutorials feature an array of exciting topics covering GNN, recommendation,
knowledge extraction, LTR, personalization, bias and unfairness in peer reviews, and anomaly
detection. For the Industry Day, we are delighted to have speakers from major technology
companies share their insights with us.
For the main technical program on days 2, 3, and 4, we continue the single-track tradition of
WSDM with 112 papers from a mix of academic and industrial speakers. We are also happy to
feature three prominent researchers as our keynote speakers: Yoav Shoham (Stanford University
and AI21 Labs), Susan Dumais (Microsoft Research), and Eran Segal (Weizmann Institute). On the
final day of the conference, the program will feature five workshops on topics including integrity
in social networks, machine reasoning, conversational systems, on-line tourism, and data-driven
decision making. Together, these activities make for a well-rounded and technically strong
program.

Preface by the Program Chairs
It is our pleasure to welcome you to WSDM, the 14th annual ACM International Conference on
Web Search and Data Mining (WSDM), virtually held in Jerusalem, Israel, March 8-12, 2021.
WSDM is one of the premier conferences on web inspired research involving search and data
mining. We are pleased to present here the proceedings of the conference. The program reflects
the breadth and diversity of research in the field and showcases the latest developments in the
field. The conference received a total of 603 submissions, the second highest number in the history
of the WSDM conference series. The submitted papers cover the research of 2209 authors across 46
countries. Of these, 112 were accepted for publication, with an acceptance rate of 18.6%. This year,
we chose to strive for a slightly higher acceptance rate, to be more inclusive in the breadth of
research featured at the conference.
We followed the double-blind review procedure adopted two years ago. In the first stage of
reviewing, four program committee members were assigned to each paper. In 8% of cases,
additional reviews were solicited, and 49 papers received as many as 5 or even 6 reviews. This
helped the SPC and the program chairs to get a diverse set of opinions to judge the merits of every
submission. The PC members provided ratings and comments while evaluating the papers
according to the standard criteria of relevance, quality, reproducibility, clarity, and impact. This
resulted in the collection of 2395 reviews. In the second stage, every paper was assigned to a Senior
PC member. The SPC member was tasked to oversee a discussion amongst the reviewers and
attempt to reach a consensus recommendation for the paper. The final decisions were based on all
of the above.
We owe a debt of gratitude to the 62 Senior PC members, 611 PC members and 240 external
reviewers who participated in this process. Some SPC and PC members went above and beyond
the call of duty, and provided truly outstanding reviews that helped us make well-informed
decisions. We recognized the work of 4 SPC (6%) and 36 PC members (also 6%) with outstanding
reviewer awards.
This year, continuing with WSDM tradition, single-track oral presentation slots were allocated to a
subset of the accepted papers. All 112 accepted papers had their recorded presentation uploaded to
the conference platform, while only 69 papers got a slot in the program. All accepted papers were
given an option to present the work as a poster. The selection of papers for oral presentation was
based on the topic and results in each paper, and their appeal to a broader audience.
The technical program this year features keynotes by prominent researchers from academia and
industry: Susan Dumais (Microsoft Research), Eran Segal (Weizmann Institute), and Yoav Shoham
(AI21 & Stanford). We would like to thank the keynote speakers for sharing their technical insights
and research contributions with the conference attendees. We are grateful to all those who
submitted papers to the conference, as well as the attendees of the conference. We would also like
to thank the general chairs, David Carmel, Liane Lewin-Eytan, and Elad Yom-Tov, who were our
partners in putting the conference program together, providing helpful feedback and advice along
the way. We hope you will find the WSDM program interesting and thought-provoking.
