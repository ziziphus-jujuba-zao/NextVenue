Message from the General and Program Chairs
The paper review process had two tracks: Applications and Algorithms.
While papers in both tracks were expected to include original, novel,
high-quality contributions, papers in the Applications track did not
necessarily require algorithmic novelty. About 77.3% of papers were
submitted to the Algorithms track, and 22.7% were submitted to the
Applications track. The acceptance rates across the two tracks were
similar: 41.7% for Algorithms and 40.9% for Applications.

Welcome to Waikoloa, on the island of Hawaiʻi, the home of the Winter
Conference on Applications of Computer Vision (WACV) for four of the
last six years! As the premier venue for research advances in applications
of computer vision, our meeting continues to grow along with the
interest and advances in AI and computer vision. For WACV 2024, we
have incorporated some new features and made some changes from
previous iterations to accommodate this growth. We hope that you enjoy
the meeting and find time to enjoy the island.

WACV 2024 consists of a three-day main conference program followed
by two days of workshops and tutorials. Following WACV tradition, each
day of the main conference starts in the afternoon, includes a keynote
presentation, and attendees engage in poster sessions after dinner. This
year, we have added a new panel on Innovation in Computer Vision and
a fireside (better yet, oceanside) chat for the Saturday keynote talk.
WACV also joins the ranks of the major vision conferences with Ralph
Anzarouth from Computer Vision News delivering a daily magazine with
updates and highlights from the proceedings. On Saturday evening, the
conference culminates with a luau.
We continue to attract a large number of submissions, reaching a record
2,042 valid submissions – around 30% more than WACV 2023. To handle
this record number of submissions, we relied on 190 Area Chairs and
1,841 reviewers. We are especially grateful to the reviewers who were
willing to conduct last-minute emergency reviews, ensuring that all
papers received at least 3 reviews. The reviewing process was doubleblind and followed best practices to avoid conflicts of interest. After
receiving the reviews, the Area Chairs made decisions in groups of two,
so that each paper was considered by multiple reviewers and multiple
Area Chairs.

Aloha!

We continued the WACV tradition of a two-round submission process. By
the Round 1 deadline in mid-June, we received 817 valid paper
submissions. Of these, 91 were accepted, and the remaining were invited
to be revised and resubmitted to Round 2, along with a rebuttal. Of the
revised papers, 282 were accepted and 276 rejected, while the remaining
168 were withdrawn. The overall acceptance rate for Round 1 was
therefore 45.6%. Authors could also submit new papers directly to Round
2, which had a deadline in late August. We received 1,226 valid
submissions in Round 2 of which 473 (38.6%) were accepted. Overall,
across both rounds WACV received a total of 2,043 paper submissions
and accepted a total of 846 papers, for an overall acceptance rate of
41.4%. These statistics illustrate how WACV’s innovative two-round
submission process allows authors to incorporate reviewer feedback to
improve their papers (in Round 1), leading to a higher acceptance rate, as
well as receiving quick feedback for Round 2.
