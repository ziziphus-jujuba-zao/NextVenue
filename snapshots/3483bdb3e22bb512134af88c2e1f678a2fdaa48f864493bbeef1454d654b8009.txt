Message from the WSDM 2022 Chairs
We are delighted to welcome you to the 15th ACM International Conference on Web Search and
Data Mining (WSDM) 2022. While originally scheduled to be held in Tempe, Arizona, the
conference had to move in late December 2021 to a virtual format. This difficult decision was made
in light of the surge in Omicron cases, enhanced travel restrictions from various parts of the world,
and companies scrapping office-return deadlines without setting a new date.
WSDM (pronounced “wisdom”) is one of the premier conferences on web-inspired research
involving search and data mining. WSDM publishes original, high-quality papers related to search
and data mining on the Web and the Social Web, with an emphasis on practical yet principled
novel models of search and data mining, algorithm design and analysis, economic implications, and
in-depth experimental analysis of accuracy and performance. This year’s conference hosts an
exciting technical program, including invited talks, as well as refereed full papers. The conference
hosts an exciting technical program, including 7 workshops, 7 tutorials, 9 doctoral consortium
presentations, 14 technical demos, 160 research papers, industry day presentations, smart city day
presentations, and 3 exciting keynote talks on timely topics:


Yejin Choi (University of Washington) - Knowledge is Power: Symbolic Knowledge
Distillation, Commonsense Morality, and Multimodal Script Knowledge



George Karypis (Amazon, University of Minnesota) - Graph Neural Network Research at
AWS AI



Ricardo Baeza-Yates (Northeastern University) - Ethics Challenges in AI

We are immensely grateful to the many researchers and volunteers who have shaped the
conference program. In particular, we thank the authors, presenters, and the WSDM’22 program
committee. We also acknowledge the Executive Committees of the four ACM SIGs, SIGIR,
SIGKDD, SIGMOD, and SIGWEB, and the members of the WSDM Steering Committee. The
committee chair Jian Pei deserves special thanks for providing us with his advice at all stages of
the conference organization.
We are also grateful to the CMT and EasyChair teams for their extremely prompt and helpful
support throughout the various aspects of the complex conference reviewing process, as well as to
Whova and Zoom, which together helped us run a successful virtual international conference. We
also give our most sincere thanks to the ACM and Sheridan for their immense help in preparing
the proceedings for publication.
We thank SIGIR and the National Science Foundation for their student author registration awards.
We also acknowledge Arizona State University and the conference’s industrial sponsors for their
invaluable supports.
We sincerely hope that you will enjoy the conference.
