Submission Categories (Presentation Formats)

With all presentation formats, presenters must present on-site at the Convention in Seattle, Washington.

Expand to learn more about each Submission Category.

Symposium+

A focused session in which individual speakers present their research on a common topic. Symposia should have the dual goals of providing diversity of perspective and integrating those perspectives into a meaningful whole.

 * Composition: 1 Chair, 1 Co-Chair (optional), 1 Discussant (optional), and 3–4 Presenters, with up to 15 Co-Authors for each presenter
 * Session Format: Scheduled in 80-minute sessions, inclusive of discussion and Q&A among presenters and the audience.
 * If the symposium is not accepted, individual presentations can be considered for a poster presentation.

Submitted Talk+

 A 15-minute individual presentation in a particular area of psychological science.

 * Length: Submitted Talks are 15 minutes total, inclusive of the presentation with slides and audience Q&A.
 * Session Format: Submitted Talks will be grouped to form a cohesive session around a common theme and scheduled consecutively within 80-minute sessions.
 * Submitted Talks that are not accepted will be considered for a poster presentation.

Flash Talk+

A 5-minute individual presentation with slides delivered by a primary researcher in a particular area of psychological science.

 * Length: Flash Talks are 5 minutes with additional time for brief audience Q&A (1-2 questions).
 * Session Format: Flash Talks will be grouped around a common theme and scheduled consecutively within 50-minute sessions.
 * Flash Talks that are not accepted will be considered for a poster presentation.

Standard Poster+

Standard Posters offer an informal, interactive presentation format. Presenters display their work on a physical poster and engage in one-on-one or small-group conversations. This format encourages dialogue, networking, and in-depth discussion of your work.

 * Session Format: Accepted Posters are grouped by topic and scheduled into 50-minute poster sessions.
 * Please note: Only Standard Posters are eligible for Poster Awards.
 * Size: 4 feet in height by 8 feet in width (48 inches in height by 96 inches in width), readable from 3-4 feet with summary.
 * Orientation: Landscape orientation (no portrait posters). Please see the Sample Poster Configuration Image here.
 * Please bring printed poster onsite, no final poster file required in advance. All fastening materials will be provided by APS.

Pilot Project Poster+

Pilot Project Posters offer the opportunity to share initial findings, gather feedback, and refine methodology before full-scale implementation, or to receive early feedback on a research plan. Presenters are encouraged to discuss challenges, next steps, and potential directions for future work.

Pilot Project Posters may have some data collection completed; a plan for a future research study where data collection has not yet begun; OR an established analytic plan where the analysis has not yet begun.

 * Session Format: Accepted Posters are grouped by topic and scheduled into 50-minute poster sessions.
 * Size: 4 feet in height by 8 feet in width (48 inches in height by 96 inches in width), readable from 3-4 feet with summary.
 * Orientation: Landscape orientation (no portrait posters). Please see the Sample Poster Configuration Image here.
 * Please bring printed poster onsite, no final poster file required in advance. All fastening materials will be provided by APS.

Teaching Poster+

Teaching posters should pertain to the teaching of psychological science. This includes teaching techniques, innovations, evaluation, and philosophy. Posters should focus on methods for teaching psychology, such as particularly effective or innovative courses or course organizations, strategies (including demonstrations) for promoting active learning, ways of integrating course material, helpful use of technology, and the like.

 * Session Format: These posters are presented as part of the pre-conference Teaching Institute, which takes place on Thursday.
 * Please note: The Teaching Institute requires an additional registration fee.
 * Size: 4 feet in height by 8 feet in width (48 inches in height by 96 inches in width), readable from 3–4 feet with summary.
 * Orientation: Landscape orientation (no portrait posters). Please see the Sample Poster Configuration Image here.
 * Please bring printed poster onsite, no final poster file required in advance. All fastening materials will be provided by APS.

Submission Types  – New for 2027!

For each submission category, presenters may submit one of the following submission types. Take note of the required information for each submission type. All submissions should reflect original research in any area of psychology conducted by the presenting author.

With the exception of Pilot Project Posters, data collection and analysis must be completed by the time of submission. The research should not have been presented or published before October 15, 2026. Pre-registered research is especially encouraged.

Expand to learn more about each Submission Type.

Empirical Research+

Submissions should present empirical research addressing a clearly defined psychological question using quantitative, qualitative, or mixed methods approaches. Specify hypotheses, study design, sampling, analytic methods, and key results.

Systematic Review+

Submissions should present transparent, reproducible syntheses of the literature on a defined question. Preference given to work addressing theoretically significant questions, knowledge gaps, resolving conflicting findings, or informing research, practice, or policy. Specify the research question, eligibility criteria, search strategy, approach to bias, and key findings. If the systematic review also includes a meta-analysis or meta-synthesis, submit under “Meta-Analysis and Meta-Synthesis”.

Meta-Analysis and Meta-Synthesis+

Submissions should present quantitative meta-analyses or qualitative meta-syntheses integrating findings across studies. Preference given to work addressing theoretically significant questions, knowledge gaps, resolving conflicting findings, or informing research, practice, or policy. Specify the research questions, search protocol, analytic methods, approach to bias, and key findings.

Methods and Applications+

Submissions should address the development, evaluation, or application of research methods in psychological science (e.g., measurement, experimental design, statistical modeling, computational approaches). Critical evaluations of existing practices and interdisciplinary contributions are welcome. Clearly articulate the methodological contribution, supporting evidence, limitations, and relevance to the field.

Subject Areas and Keywords

All submitters will select one subject area from the Subject Area List and enter up to 3 open-ended keywords (at least 1 required). The chosen subject area and keywords will be used to assign each submission to an AI-generated Program Theme. Please see the 2026 Program Themes.
