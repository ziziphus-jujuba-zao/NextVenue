WSDM 2025 General Chairs’ Welcome
Welcome to the 18th ACM International Conference on Web and Data Mining, this time taking
place in Hannover, Germany.
Many volunteers have helped to provide excellent content for the conference, most of all the 3 PC
Chairs, Meeyoung Cha (Max Planck Institute for Security and Privacy in Germany), Francine
Moens (KU Leuven, Belgium) and Marc Najork (Google DeepMind, USA), who selected more than
100 papers from over 600 submissions for presentation at the conference.
This exciting WSDM 2025 main conference program was extended by interesting demonstrations
like Lightning IR for fine-tuning and inference of transformer-based language models for
information retrieval, WildlifeLookup, a chatbot designed to facilitate wildlife management and
Ventana a la Verdad, a chatbot for navigating Colombian civil conflict archives. Several workshops
ranging from language models to trust and verification on the Web complemented this program
and provided important opportunities for the discussion of exciting new ideas and approaches.
WSDM Cup 2025 challenged more than 1000 competitors to create useful and efficient auto-rater
models that can accurately reflect human evaluations. This competition focused on the creation of
multilingual auto-rater models using chatbot arena data, with exciting solutions. The WSDM 2025
Industry Day featured talks from leading companies on practical applications of web search and
data mining. Some notable examples included zero-shot image moderation in Google Ads,
advancing Voice AI for E-commerce at Amazon, and Fact-checking of multilingual podcasts.
Finally, WSDM Day talks focused on medical research questions and topics, applying language
models, knowledge graphs and other AI based approaches.
For participants aiming to enter new research areas, the WSDM 2025 tutorials covered a broad
range of topics, including Robust Information Retrieval, Building Trustworthy AI Models for
Medicine, Unifying Bias and Unfairness in Information Retrieval in the LLM Era, Advances in
Vector Search and quite a few more. And finally, three excellent invited speakers (Slav Petrov from
Google DeepMind and co-lead on Gemini, Roberto Navigli from Sapienza University and
Babelscape and Mario Fritz from CISPA) provided exciting insights into the state of AI, about what
large language models really understand, as well as privacy and security aspects of neural models.
Our special thanks go to our WSDM 2025 Team, including Sandipan Sikdar, coordinating many of
the organizational tasks and to our many WSDM volunteers helping before and during the
conference!
We hope you enjoyed the conference like we did and enjoy browsing the WSDM 2025 proceedings
and are looking forward to meeting all of you for the next WSDM 2026 Conference and related
events!

Message from the Program Chairs
It is our great pleasure to welcome you to the 18th ACM International Conference on Web Search
and Data Mining (WSDM 2025), taking place in Hannover (Germany) from March 10 to March 14,
2025. WSDM is one of the premier conferences in the fields of web search and web data mining,
with a strong and active participation from both academia and industry. Over the last few years,
these fields have been transformed by the advent of generative language models. Many of the
papers contained in the WSDM 2025 proceedings leverage these new technologies. These are
exciting times for the field!
In order to produce a technical program of the highest quality, we recruited 105 senior program
committee members, each with a distinguished track record in the field, and 466 program
committee members. We tried our best to select a committee that is representative of the
community, in terms of gender, location, affiliation, and career stage. Each senior program
committee member was responsible for at least four submissions, with the majority (82 of 105)
handling six submissions. Regular program committee members were assigned at least four
reviews, with the vast majority receiving six or seven assignments.
The WSDM 2025 research track received 628 paper submissions. 14 of these submissions were
withdrawn within the first few days of the review period. Of the remaining submissions, 8 were
desk-rejected for a variety of reasons, including corrupted PDF files, breaches of the anonymity
requirements, and formatting and length issues. The remaining 606 submissions went through a
rigorous review process. Each submission was assigned to five program committee members under
the supervision of one senior program committee member. We chose to over-provision the
reviewing pool so as to cope with some reviews not being completed in a timely fashion. 50
submissions received the minimum number of three reviews, 201 submissions received four
reviews, and 355 submissions received all five reviews. The review period was followed by a ten
day discussion period, moderated by the senior program committee members. Finally, the three
program chairs met online with senior program combers to discuss those submissions that were
near the accept/reject boundary. We accepted 106 of the 628 original submissions, for an
acceptance rate of 16.9%. One accepted paper was subsequently withdrawn, resulting in 105 papers
in the proceedings. 40 of these 105 accepted papers were selected for a plenary presentation, and
the remaining 65 for a poster presentation. Plenary and poster presentations were organized into
nine sessions, spread over the three days of the main conference.
A special thanks to the best paper selection committee, composed of eight senior members of the
community and chaired by Yoelle Maarek and Elad Yom-Tov. Each of the 20 highest-rated
submissions was reviewed by 3 to 4 members of that committee, leading up to the selection of this
year’s best paper.
We hope that attendees of WSDM 2025 will find the presentations stimulating and thoughtprovoking, and that a fair number of the papers in these proceedings will be viewed as seminal
publications in years to come!
