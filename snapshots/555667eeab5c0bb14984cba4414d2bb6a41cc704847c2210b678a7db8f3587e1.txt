Foreword from the General Chairs
It is our great pleasure to welcome you to The ACM Web Conference 2024 held in person with virtual
components on May 13-17, 2024, in Singapore. It is the 33rd edition of a series of yearly international
conferences on the future directions of the Web. This is also the first time that the conference is held in
Singapore, a sunny tropical island full of possibilities!
The Web Conference, formerly known as International World Wide Web Conference and abbreviated
as WWW, began the journey in 1994 at CERN. This conference has been the premier venue to present
and discuss progress in research, development, standards and applications of topics related to the Web.
The Web Conference has been the forum where some of the most fundamental Web technologies have
been introduced, such as the Anatomy of a Large-Scale Web Search Engine in 1998, prefiguring Google,
the EigenTrust algorithm in 2003, influencing reputation management distributed networks, and the
YAGO knowledge base in 2007, anticipating the numerous developments over knowledge graphs.
The conference has one unifying goal: to envision and create the future of the Web. In the last three
decades, the scholars, researchers, practitioners, policymakers and commercial ventures came to this
conference to present research results, discuss the evolution of Web, and the impact of Web technologies
on society and culture. Today, the conference also embraces the elements of AI, LLM (Large Language
Models) and multimodality into the Web. There will be more debate on how to integrate these elements
into the Web technologies for the creation of a fair and inclusive Web development ecology.
The program of this year’s edition includes four distinguished keynote talks by the world-renowned
experts: Bo Zhang and Jie Tang from Tsinghua University, Jon Kleinberg from Cornell University, Bin Liu
from National University of Singapore, and Jeannie Marie Paterson from The University of Melbourne.
The high-quality program is composed of 16 workshops, 20 tutorials, research tracks with oral
presentations and poster sessions, industry tracks with oral and poster presentations, short paper poster
and demo sessions, a resource track, a panel and a PhD symposium. In addition, we have two special
tracks (Web4Good, History of the Web) and three special days (Online Trust and Safety Day, Large
Language Model Day, and Health Day). Following the tradition, we have the Web4All as a co-located
conference, highlighting the latest research results in Web accessibility. We also have a journal track built
in coordination with the TWEB journal, which offers the opportunity for authors of the three selected
journal papers to come and present their works at the conference.
The various calls for papers have attracted a record number of submissions from all over the world, which
have been thoroughly reviewed by a very large program committee. We are very grateful to Ravi Kumar
from Google and Hady W. Lauw from Singapore Management University, our amazing program chairs
who dedicated so much of their time and efforts to this conference. We would also like to thank the entire
Organizing Committee, and all the program committee members, especially the senior area chairs, who
work very hard in selecting high quality papers for this conference.
We wish to thank ACM SIGWEB, all our supporters (Zhipu AI, TII, TikTok, Google, Baidu, and Singapore
Tourism Board) for their support, and our institutional partner, Singapore Management University, for
providing the manpower to administer conference registration, venue logistics and social events
organization. All their contributions and hard work make the 2024 edition an outstanding conference.

Foreword from the Program Chairs
The Research Program of The Web Conference this year consists of ten tracks, each one of them
managed by a group of two to three track chairs, who were tasked with defining the scope of their
track, recruiting area chairs and reviewers, overseeing the reviewing process, and proposing a set of
papers for inclusion in the proceedings.
TRACKS
We revised the tracks from last year, considering contemporary research trends in the Web community.
The call for papers attracted 2008 submissions, a 6.2% increase from last year. After the reviewing
process, 405 papers were accepted (20.2%), which you can find in these proceedings; 189 of these were
selected for oral presentation. The program of the conference also features three papers selected by
ACM TWEB among the best submissions to their journal. In addition, all papers are given an
opportunity for poster presentation.
PROCESS SYNOPSIS
For the first time, we used the OpenReview system for managing the reviewing process. The Program
Committee comprised 31 track chairs, 125 area chairs, and 1,563 reviewers. Each paper was reviewed by
at least 3 reviewers; the majority received 4 to 5 reviews. Each reviewer had an average load of 6
papers, and a maximum load of 8 papers. After reviews, authors had the opportunity for rebuttal, with
back-and-forth discussion allowed for a two-week period. Area chairs made recommendations, taking
into account reviews and discussions. Finally, the track chairs produced per-track recommendations
(including oral presentation recommendations) that were collectively discussed with us. Submissions by
the track chairs were managed in a separate COI track that we chaired, but using a similar reviewing
process. Eventually, the submission versions of the accepted papers, as well as their accompanying
reviews, will be released on OpenReview. This is in addition to the archival, camera-ready versions
indexed by the ACM Digital Library.
BEST PAPER
The selection process began with nominations from the track chairs. The nominees were further
whittled down by the program chairs to a shortlist of candidates, taking into account topic diversity.
The final selection was conducted by an independent committee comprising eminent researchers in the
community. The Best Paper Award will be announced at the conference.
OUTSTANDING REVIEWERS
While we are grateful to all reviewers, we are particularly heartened by the reviewers who spared no
effort in providing high-quality reviews as well as participating actively in the discussions during the
rebuttal process. With the help of area chairs who rated the quality of each review, we identified 40
reviewers, whom we recognize as Outstanding Reviewers, to be featured on the conference homepage
as well as the proceedings.
ARTIFACT BADGING
We continued to encourage accepted papers to qualify for the Artifact Available badge by releasing
code, datasets, or similar artifacts through publicly available, archival repositories providing a
