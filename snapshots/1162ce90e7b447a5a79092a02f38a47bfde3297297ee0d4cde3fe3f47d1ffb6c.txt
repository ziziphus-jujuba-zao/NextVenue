Abstract
The 16th ACM International Conference on Web Search and Data Mining (WSDM 2023)
was held in Singapore. It was held as in-person conference that also featured rich virtual
elements. This brief report provides an overview of WSDM 2023 with organization and
program details and statistics from Conference Chairs and Program Committee Chairs, as
well as a message from the WSDM Steering Committee Chair.
Date: 27 February–3 March 2023.
Website: https://www.wsdm-conference.org/2023.

1

Introduction

WSDM (pronounced ”wisdom”) is one of the premier conferences on web-inspired research involving search and data mining. WSDM publishes original, high-quality papers related to search
and data mining on the Web and the Social Web, with an emphasis on practical yet principled
novel models of search and data mining, algorithm design and analysis, economic implications,
and in-depth experimental analysis of accuracy and performance.
After the previous two years of virtual conferences, the 2023 edition was an in-person conference, and we were excited to meet many colleagues face-to-face again. Meanwhile, we still
supported rich virtual elements via Whova and Zoom to accommodate attendees that might have
faced travel restrictions or financial inadequacies as many parts of the world were in the process
of opening up as the organizational effort was underway.
The full conference took place over 5 days, featuring a rich and wide-ranging program. The
first day saw the presentation of 10 tutorials, as well as the Industry Day. The middle 3-day

Program

Overall, 123 papers were accepted out of 690 submissions, at the acceptance rate of 17.8%. Compared to the previous two years, WSDM 2022 with 788 submissions and acceptance rate of 20.5%
and WSDM 2021 with 603 submissions and 18.6% acceptance rate, WSDM 2023 maintained comparable numbers of submissions and acceptance rates. We looked at the distribution of submitted
as well as accepted papers by the country domain of the corresponding authors, as shown in
Figure 1. The top five leading countries in terms of number of submissions were China, United
States, Australia, South Korea and India. The top five leading countries in terms of number of
accepted papers were China, United States, Australia, India and Singapore.
To illustrate the dominant subject areas, Figure 2 shows a word cloud of the title words of
accepted papers. Some of the major keywords include recommendation, retrieval, ranking, which
reflect the traditional areas of interest of the SIGIR community, including the WSDM conference.
Out of the 123 research papers, 52 were presented across 9 oral sessions as follows: 1) Social Issues in Data Mining; 2) Recommender Systems I; 3) Graph Neural Networks; 4) Best of
WSDM 2023; 5) Recommender Systems II; 6) Learning; 7) Graph Mining; 8) Recommendation
and Learning; 9) Language Models and Text Mining.

In particular, the “Best of WSDM 2023” featured in-person presentations by the best paper
candidates [Pougué-Biyong et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2023; Cai et al., 2023; Xuan et al., 2023] as
well as the video presentation by the Test of Time Award [Backstrom and Leskovec, 2011].
The other 71 research papers were presented as posters during the poster reception. There
were also two half-day demo sessions that ran parallel to the oral sessions.
Some tutorials on Day 1 were on topics relevant to retrieval, including trustworthy algorithmic
ranking systems, proactive conversational agents, and AutoML for deep recommender systems.
The WSDM Cup featured a number of competitions on IR topics. Baidu sponsored challenges
on unbiased learning to rank and pre-training for Web search. In turn, University of Waterloo
held competitions on multilingual information retrieval along two tracks, namely known languages
and surprise languages. The last challenge held by Toloka was on visual question answering. The
finalists presented their winning solutions on Day 5 of the conference during the WSDM Cup Day.
Further details of the papers and other interesting works can be found in the proceedings1
[Chua et al., 2023]. The talks (including the keynotes) can be found on ACM’s YouTube channel2 .

Hybrid Mode

The conference was run in a two-way hybrid mode, allowing both remote presentations and virtual
attendance. The major events such as keynote talks, oral sessions, workshops, tutorials were livestreamed in real time via Zoom. We also accommodated remote presentations, 46% or the oral
presentations and 39% of the posters respectively.

Considering all participants, including authors, registrants, sponsor representatives, volunteers,
as well as complimentary registrations provided to invited speakers, panellists, and key committee
members, the conference involved altogether 471 attendees, out of which 344 (73%) were present
in person and 127 (27%) virtually. Figure 3 shows the geographic distribution of attendees.
The overall fraction of students was about 40% of attendees. These include 9 student presenters supported by the SIGIR Student Travel Grant Program, 10 US-based student registrants
supported by NSF Student Travel Awards. Moreover, there were 14 students from economically
developing countries (Vietnam, India, Brazil, Morocco) attending virtually, supported by SIGWEB Fair Access Student Participation Awards.

Call for Bids for Future Conferences

The WSDM conference series retained a robust participation through the virtual conferences of
2021 and 2022, and it has been wonderful to see the high levels of in-person participation and
engagement in the 2023 conference in Singapore. For 2024, the conference will be back in the
Americas, in our first Latin American location: Mérida, Mexico. We are currently taking bids for
WSDM 2025 in Europe, and WSDM 2026 in the Americas. See the Call for Bids3 for details, or
feel free to get in touch with the Steering Committee at wsdm-sc@googlegroups.com.
