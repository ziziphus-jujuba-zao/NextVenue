Foreword from the General Chairs
It is our great pleasure to welcome you to The ACM Web Conference 2023 held in person with
virtual components on April 30 - May 4 2023 in Austin, Texas, USA. It is the 32st edition of a
series of yearly international conferences on the future directions of the Web. This is the first time
we get back in person after the COVID pandemic. Let’s meet in person and celebrate together!
Since the invention of the World Wide Web in 1989, The Web Conference (formerly known as
International World Wide Web Conference, abbreviated as WWW) is a yearly international
academic conference on the topic of the future direction of the World Wide Web. This conference
has been the premier venue to present and discuss progress in research, development, standards,
and applications of the topics related to the Web. Over the past three decades, The Web Conference
has been the forum where some of the most fundamental Web technologies have been introduced,
such as the Anatomy of a Large Scale Web Search Engine in 1998 prefiguring Google, the
EigenTrust algorithm in 2003 influencing reputation management distributed networks, and the
YAGO knowledge base in 2007 anticipating the numerous developments over knowledge graphs.
The Conference aims to provide the world with a premier forum for discussion and debate about
the evolution of the Web, the standardization of its associated technologies, and the impact of those
technologies on society and culture. The conference brings together scholars, researchers,
developers, policymakers, users and commercial ventures — indeed all those who are passionate
about the Web and what it has to offer - with one unifying goal: to envision and create the future
of the Web.
The program of this year’s edition includes six distinguished keynote talks by world-renowned
experts: Bob Metcalfe (MIT and 2022 ACM A.M. Turing Award), Barbara Poblete (University of
Chile and Amazon), David Rand (MIT), Payal Arora (Erasmus University Rotterdam), Zachary
Elkins (University of Texas at Austin) and Michalis Vazirgiannis (École Polytechnique).
The high-quality program is made of 364 selected papers spread in 11 research sessions, in addition
to posters and demonstrations, a PhD symposium for the junior scholars, an Industry track, a
Developer track for the practitioners as well as 27 tutorials and 21 workshops reflecting the novel
topics of interests for the Web.
A journal track built in coordination with the TWEB journal also offers the possibility to authors
of 3 journal papers to come and present their work to the participants.
The scientific team came up with a new special track on The Creative Web and continued with
special tracks of Web for good as well as a track discussing the History of the Web, its evolution
and impact.
Following the tradition, the W4A (Web for All) conference is a co-located event highlighting the
importance of researching methods and tools to build an accessible Web. We took it to the next
level and co-located two more events: Web Science (WebSci) and Provenance Week.
New to this year, we came up with three Special Days events that bring together invited
presentations on the topics of Health, Knowledge Graphs and Entrepreneurs.
The various calls for papers have attracted a record number of submissions from all over the world,
which have been thoroughly reviewed by a very large program committee. We are very grateful to

Foreword from the Program Chairs
The Research Program of The Web Conference this year consists of eleven tracks, each one of
them managed by a group of three track chairs, who were tasked with defining the scope of their
track, recruiting program committee members, steering the reviewing process, and proposing a set
of papers for inclusion in the proceedings.
This year, we continued a multi-year process of defining more precisely what is a "Web paper":
a paper that directly engages with the Web as a technical infrastructure or as a socio-economic
system, or with Web technologies, standards, or platforms. We also considered that a "Web paper"
is one that addresses the impact of the Web and the way in which it is accessible and accountable
to a broader audience. As part of this process, we asked track chairs to create a call for papers
articulating a sharper vision of the scope of their track, and to check whether each paper submitted
to their track was within that scope.
We introduced a new track on Fairness, Accountability, Transparency, and Ethics on the Web
and would like to thank Alexandra Olteanu, Michael Ekstrand, and Krishna Gummadi for accepting
to serve as its inaugural track chairs.
The call for papers attracted 1900 submissions, a 4% increase from last year. Track chairs
considered that 117 papers were out of scope for their track and for the conference, and those papers
received a "desk reject" notification. After the reviewing process, 365 papers were accepted
(19.2%), which you can find in these proceedings. The program of the conference also features
three papers selected by ACM TWEB among the best submissions to their journal.
The scale of the effort leading to these proceedings is enormous: 35 track chairs, 254 senior PC
members, and 1,325 PC members. Each paper was reviewed by 3-4 PC members drawn from a
program committee of 1,325 experts. Then, an online discussion was steered by a senior PC
member, drawn from a senior program committee of 254 experts. Finally, the 35 track chairs
produced per-track recommendations that were collectively discussed with the 3 program chairs.
We would like to thank all those involved for their commitment and dedication.
In addition to continuing the process of sharpening the scope of the research program, and
introducing a new track, we are also proud of a further innovation. We piloted the use of a badge
for papers releasing a research artifact in ACM’s Digital Library. We asked authors to release
code, datasets, or similar artifacts through publicly available, archival repositories for research
artifacts providing a Document Object Identifier (DOI). A committee led by Natasha Noy reviewed
over 70 accepted papers that applied for the "Artifacts Available" badge.
Together with this and next year's organization committee, we look forward to receiving your
feedback about the research program and these proceedings. For us, serving such a dedicated and
diverse research community has been a pleasure.
Sincerely, the Web Conference 2023 Research Program co-chairs.
